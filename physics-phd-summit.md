There is an urgent need for the physics community to address the impact of AI on physics PhD education. Inspired by the [recent white paper](https://cmsa.fas.harvard.edu/media/2026/09/Summit-on-PhD-Math-Education-in-the-Age-of-AI.pdf) released by the math community on this topic, IAIFI (with funding from the MIT School of Science and Department of Physics) will host a 2-day Summit on November 16–17, 2026, at MIT in Cambridge, MA. The Summit will invite diverse perspectives from leading physics departments at private and public universities in the U.S. with a goal of releasing a whitepaper shortly thereafter. This Summit is invite-only.

* When: November 16–17, 2026 
* Where: MIT

## Working Groups

Participants will be organized into the following three working groups: 

- **Learning:** What are best practices for graduate coursework and graduate student independent learning? How can we adjust teaching methods and goals to promote learning?
- **Research:** What are recommended practices for graduate student use and non-use of AI in research? How do we train graduate students to be efficient users of AI in research, and how do we guard against skill loss? Which skills must be preserved?
- **Assessment:** How should graduate students be assessed in program requirements beyond coursework?

## Preparation
- Each representative should [complete this form](https://app.smartsheet.com/b/form/01a145538bb47a2ca292f881a30211e1) to book accommodations and communicate their working group preference.
-  We invite each institution to prepare one slide to share on the first day of the Summit. This could include experiences or innovations at your institution, results of a survey of your students or faculty, or any other perspective you would like to share.
